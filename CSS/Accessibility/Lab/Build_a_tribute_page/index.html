Terry A. Davis

The man who single-handedly built TempleOS

Here's a timeline of Terry A. Davis's life:

  • 1969 — Born December 15 in West Allis, Wisconsin
  • As a teenager (1980s) - Learns assembly language on a Commodore 64
  • 1994 - Receives a Master's degree in Electrical Engineering from Arizona State University
  • 1990–1996 — Works as a programmer and hardware engineer at Ticketmaster on VAX systems
  • 1996 - Suffers manic episodes and a profound revelation he described as coming from God; diagnosed with schizophrenia (initially bipolar); begins years of hospitalizations while still pursuing technical work
  • 1997–2003 - Holds engineering roles at companies like Xytec Corp. and Graphic Technologies; starts his own venture (H.A.R.E.)
  • 2003–2005 - Leaves traditional employment and begins developing his operating system full-time, first called J Operating System
  • 2006–2012 - Renames the project LoseThos and dedicates himself completely to it
  • 2003–2013 - Single-handedly writes more than 100,000–120,000 lines of code in his custom language HolyC; builds a complete 64-bit public-domain operating system from scratch (kernel, compiler, editor, games including After Egypt, 3D graphics, flight simulator, and a random oracle for "talking to God"), following what he believed were divine instructions for 640×480 resolution and 16 colors
  • 2013 - Renames it TempleOS and announces "God's temple is finished"
  • 2010s - Uploads video blogs explaining his creation, gaining a dedicated following in the programming community; TempleOS is later exhibited as outsider art in France (2017)
  • 2018 - Dies at age 48 on August 11 after being struck by a train in The Dalles, Oregon

"What people are going to read is, 'It's about a pathetic schizophrenic who made a crappy operating system.' My perspective is, 'God said I made His temple.'"

-- Terry A. Davis
